# Debt Elimination Strategies: Snowball vs. Avalanche

Eliminating non-mortgage consumer debt, particularly high-interest credit cards, is one of the highest guaranteed return-on-investment actions an individual can undertake. Two proven methodologies dominate personal finance: the Debt Avalanche and the Debt Snowball.

## 1. The Debt Avalanche Method (Mathematically Optimal)
In the Debt Avalanche framework, you prioritize debts strictly by interest rate (Annual Percentage Rate, APR):
1. List all debts ordered from highest interest rate to lowest interest rate, regardless of outstanding balance.
2. Maintain minimum required payments on every single debt to protect credit score and avoid late penalties.
3. Funnel every surplus discretionary dollar into the debt bearing the single highest APR.
4. Once the highest-rate debt is completely paid off, roll its entire monthly allocation into the debt with the next highest interest rate.

Mathematical Benefit: The Debt Avalanche minimizes the total dollar amount paid in interest over time and clears debts in the fastest chronological duration from a pure numbers perspective.

## 2. The Debt Snowball Method (Behaviorally and Psychologically Driven)
Developed and popularized by Dave Ramsey, the Debt Snowball prioritizes emotional momentum and behavioral wins:
1. List all debts in ascending order based solely on balance amount, from smallest balance to largest balance, ignoring interest rates.
2. Pay minimum payments across all debts.
3. Attack the smallest balance with maximum intensity until it reaches zero.
4. Celebrate the milestone, then roll the minimum payment and extra cash from that cleared debt into the next smallest balance.

Psychological Benefit: Knocking out entire accounts quickly produces dopamine rewards and tangible proof of progress, reducing burnout and preventing people from quitting their debt-free journey prematurely.

## Good Debt vs. Bad Debt
- Good Debt: Incurred to acquire appreciating assets or enhance long-term earning potential at low, manageable interest rates (e.g., sensible educational student loans, reasonable fixed-rate residential mortgages, business development loans).
- Bad Debt: Incurred to purchase rapidly depreciating consumer goods or services at high interest rates (e.g., revolving credit card debt at 20%+ APR, payday loans, high-interest auto financing on luxury vehicles). Bad debt systematically erodes net worth.
