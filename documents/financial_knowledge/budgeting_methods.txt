# Budgeting Strategies: 50/30/20, Zero-Based, and Pay-Yourself-First

A budget is a deliberate financial blueprint designed to balance income against expenses and direct capital toward short-term security and long-term financial freedom.

## 1. The 50/30/20 Rule
Popularized by Senator Elizabeth Warren in "All Your Worth," the 50/30/20 framework divides after-tax net income into three distinct buckets:
- 50% for Needs: Essential living expenses required for survival and contractually required payments. Includes rent or mortgage, basic groceries, utilities, health insurance, minimum debt obligations, and vital transportation.
- 30% for Wants: Discretionary expenses that enhance life satisfaction but are not strictly vital. Includes dining out, vacations, streaming services, designer apparel, concerts, hobbies, and upgrade purchases.
- 20% for Savings and Extra Debt Repayment: Financial progress goals. Includes building an emergency fund, contributions to retirement accounts (401k, IRA), investing in index funds, and paying down principal on high-interest debts beyond required minimums.

## 2. Zero-Based Budgeting (ZBB)
In zero-based budgeting, income minus total outlays (expenses, savings, debt repayments) equals exactly zero:
Income - Outflows = $0
Every single dollar earned is deliberately assigned a specific job before the month starts. If you earn $4,000 net, every dollar is mapped across housing, groceries, utilities, emergency savings, and entertainment until zero dollars remain unassigned. This eliminates mystery leaks and forces mindfulness.

## 3. The "Pay Yourself First" Strategy (Reverse Budgeting)
Traditional budgeting tracks expenses first and saves whatever remains at the end of the month—frequently resulting in zero savings. Reverse budgeting inverts this order:
1. Immediately upon receiving your paycheck, automatically divert a predetermined percentage (e.g., 15% to 25%) directly into savings, retirement, or investments.
2. Cover required fixed expenses (rent, utilities, insurance).
3. Spend whatever is left over with zero guilt, without micro-tracking every individual coffee or receipt.

## 4. Cash Envelope System
Popularized for individuals struggling with impulse spending, this method assigns cash to high-friction categories (groceries, dining, entertainment) in labeled envelopes at the beginning of the pay cycle. Once the cash in an envelope is spent, spending in that category must stop completely until the next cycle.
