# Compound Interest and the Time Value of Money

Compound interest is the interest calculated on the initial principal and also on the accumulated interest of previous periods of a deposit or loan. Often referred to as "interest on interest," it causes wealth to grow at an exponential rate rather than a linear rate.

## The Mathematical Formula
The future value of an investment with compound interest is calculated using:
A = P * (1 + r/n)^(n*t)

Where:
- A = the future value of the investment or loan, including interest
- P = the principal investment amount (the initial deposit or loan balance)
- r = the annual nominal interest rate (in decimal form, e.g., 7% = 0.07)
- n = the number of times that interest is compounded per year (e.g., annual = 1, semi-annual = 2, quarterly = 4, monthly = 12, daily = 365)
- t = the number of years the money is invested or borrowed for

## Comparison: Simple vs Compound Interest
Simple interest is calculated exclusively on the principal amount: I = P * r * t. For example, a $10,000 principal at 5% simple annual interest yields exactly $500 each year, totalling $15,000 after 10 years.
Under compound interest compounded annually, the same $10,000 at 5% yields $500 in year one, $525 in year two, $551.25 in year three, and grows to $16,288.95 after 10 years. Over 30 years, simple interest gives $25,000, whereas compound interest compounds to $43,219.42—more than 70% higher due solely to the compounding effect.

## The Rule of 72
The Rule of 72 is a quick, handy mental shortcut to estimate the number of years required to double your invested money at a given annual rate of return:
Years to Double ≈ 72 / Annual Interest Rate

For example:
- At a 6% return: 72 / 6 = 12 years to double.
- At an 8% return: 72 / 8 = 9 years to double.
- At a 10% return: 72 / 10 = 7.2 years to double.

## Key Takeaway
The single most powerful variable in compound interest is time. Starting to save or invest even 5 or 10 years earlier drastically magnifies the compounding multiplier, even with smaller monthly contributions.
